# Empathy Portfolio
## AI-Smart Ordering System — College Canteen
### Design Thinking Phase 1: Empathise

**Project:** AI-Smart Ordering System  
**College:** Rathinam Technical Campus  
**Team Member:** Abishek  
**Date of Research:** September 2026

---

## 1. Research Overview

To understand the real problem before building anything, I spent time observing the college canteen during peak hours and conducted structured interviews with students and canteen staff. The goal was to understand **who is suffering, why, and what they actually need** — not what we assume they need.

---

## 2. Observation Log

### Session 1 — Monday, 9 September 2026
**Location:** College Canteen, Main Block  
**Time:** 12:45 PM – 1:30 PM (Lunch peak hour)  
**Observer:** Abishek  

| Time | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| 12:45 PM | Queue already 25–30 students long at the counter. Students arriving from class directly joining the end. |
| 12:52 PM | A student waited 8 minutes just to reach the counter, then was told the item he wanted (Chicken Biryani) was sold out. He left without eating. |
| 1:00 PM | Counter staff handling 3 tasks simultaneously: taking orders verbally, collecting cash, and shouting into the kitchen. Mistakes observed — wrong item given twice. |
| 1:05 PM | A group of 4 students left the canteen without buying anything because the queue was too long. Overheard: *"Machi class ku late aagum, pola"* ("Bro, we'll be late to class, let's go"). |
| 1:12 PM | Kitchen staff preparing food without knowing how many portions are needed. Saw them run out of sambar halfway through service. |
| 1:20 PM | A student was standing near the pickup counter for 15 minutes waiting for her order. She had a lab class at 1:30 PM and was visibly stressed. |
| 1:28 PM | Counter staff misheard "Veg Puff × 2" as "Veg Puff × 12". Had to redo the bill. |

**Key Observation Insight:** The queue is not just a waiting problem — it is a coordination problem. Students don't know when food will be ready, staff don't know how many to prepare, and nobody has any information about what's happening.

## 3. Interview Transcripts

### Interview 1 — Priya R., 2nd Year CSE Student
**Date:** 10 September 2026 | **Duration:** ~12 minutes  
**Setting:** Outside canteen after she came out of the queue

**Q: Can you describe what usually happens when you go to the canteen during lunch?**  
> "I have class at 1:00 PM and lunch break is only from 12:45 to 1:00 — that's 15 minutes. By the time I reach the canteen, queue is already there. I spend 10 minutes in the queue, get the food in 3 minutes, and then I have to eat while walking to class or skip eating. This happens almost every day."

**Q: What is the most frustrating part?**  
> "Not knowing. I don't know how long the queue is until I reach there. I don't know if the food I want is available. I've gone there three times this week and twice what I wanted was sold out. I still had to wait to find out."

**Q: Have you ever left without eating?**  
> "Yes, at least twice a week. I just buy biscuits from the store instead. It's sad because the canteen food is good and cheap."

**Q: If you could change one thing, what would it be?**  
> "I want to order before I leave class. Just like Swiggy. I'll place the order, go to canteen, collect it, done. No waiting."

**Q: Would you use a mobile app or website for this?**  
> "100%. Even if it's a website I'll use it. Anything is better than standing in queue."

---

### Interview 2 — Karthik S., 3rd Year ECE Student
**Date:** 10 September 2026 | **Duration:** ~10 minutes

**Q: How often do you go to the canteen?**  
> "Every day for lunch. Sometimes breakfast also if I have morning class."

**Q: What problems do you face?**  
> "The queue is the main problem. But also the uncertainty. Sometimes I wait 20 minutes and they say the item is finished. That's really frustrating. I wasted my break time for nothing."

**Q: Do you know in advance what will be available?**  
> "No. The menu is written on a board but availability — whether it's actually there — that we only know when we reach the counter."

**Q: What about the time it takes to prepare food?**  
> "That's another issue. They don't give any estimate. They just say 'wait.' I once waited 25 minutes for biryani. I had lab at 2 PM and I was late."

**Q: What would an ideal canteen experience look like?**  
> "Order in advance. Maybe during the previous class itself. Get a token number. Go to canteen at a specific time, show token, take food. No queue, no waiting. Like how they do it in some airport lounges."

---

### Interview 3 — Meena K., 1st Year IT Student
**Date:** 11 September 2026 | **Duration:** ~8 minutes

**Q: Is this your first year using the canteen?**  
> "Yes. I was surprised by how crowded it is. In school our lunch was in the classroom. Here you have to fight for food basically."

**Q: What do you find most confusing?**  
> "I don't know the preparation times. I ordered Dosa yesterday and waited 18 minutes. If I had known it would take that long I would have ordered something else."

**Q: Do you have a smartphone?**  
> "Yes, I always have it with me."

**Q: Would you use an app to pre-order food?**  
> "Definitely. I use Zomato and Swiggy at home. I know how to use these things. If the canteen had something like that I'd use it from day one."

---

### Interview 4 — Rajan (Canteen Counter Staff)
**Date:** 12 September 2026 | **Duration:** ~7 minutes  
**Note:** Interview conducted in Tamil, translated and paraphrased below.

**Q: What is your biggest challenge during peak hours?**  
> "Too many students at one time. Everyone wants different things. I have to take the order, tell the kitchen, take the money — all at once. I make mistakes sometimes. Wrong change, wrong item."

**Q: Can you predict how many portions of each item you'll need?**  
> "No. We guess. Sometimes we prepare 30 biryanis and 50 students come. Sometimes we prepare 50 and only 20 come. We waste food or we run out."

**Q: If students could order in advance, would that help?**  
> "Very much. If I know 20 students are coming for biryani at 1 PM I can prepare exactly 20. No waste, no shortage. Now we're guessing every day."

**Q: What about the kitchen team?**  
> "They also don't know. I shout the order to them and they start preparing. If 10 students order the same thing at the same time, they have to prepare 10 separate times instead of one big batch."

---

### Interview 5 — Canteen Manager (Name withheld on request)
**Date:** 12 September 2026 | **Duration:** ~10 minutes

**Q: How do you currently manage the peak hour load?**  
> "Experience. After running the canteen for 8 years I roughly know Monday lunch will be busy, Friday it will be a bit less. But within a day I can't predict. I just keep the staff ready."

**Q: What is the biggest operational problem?**  
> "Two things. One — food waste. We throw away 10–15% of what we prepare because we overprepare to avoid shortage. Two — student complaints. They complain about waiting but there's nothing I can do if everyone comes at the same time."

**Q: Would a digital pre-ordering system help?**  
> "If students use it, yes. I can plan better. Tell the kitchen exactly how many of each item in the next 30 minutes. Reduce waste. Reduce waiting. But students must use it consistently."

## 5. Customer Journey Map

**User:** Priya R., 2nd Year CSE (Typical student with tight schedule)  
**Scenario:** Going to canteen during 15-minute lunch break

| Stage | Action | Thought | Feeling | Pain Point |
|-------|--------|---------|---------|------------|
| **1. Decision** | Finishes class at 12:45 PM, decides to go canteen | "Is it worth going? Will I make it in time?" | Anxious | No way to know queue status before going |
| **2. Travel** | Walks 3–4 minutes to canteen | "Hope it's not crowded today" | Hopeful | Wastes precious break time walking |
| **3. Queue** | Joins queue of 20+ students | "This will take too long" | Frustrated | 8–15 min wait with no information |
| **4. Counter** | Reaches counter, orders Chicken Biryani | "Please let it be available" | Tense | Told it's sold out — finds out only after waiting |
| **5. Fallback** | Orders second-choice item (Dal Rice) | "Not what I wanted but okay" | Disappointed | No advance visibility into availability |
| **6. Wait** | Waits additional 7 minutes for food to be prepared | "I'm going to be late" | Stressed | No preparation time estimate given |
| **7. Eat** | Rushes to eat, food is lukewarm | "I have to go in 3 minutes" | Rushed | Can't eat properly |
| **8. Class** | Arrives 5 minutes late to next class | "This happens every day" | Defeated | Recurring problem with no solution |

**Emotional Curve:** Anxious → Hopeful → Frustrated → Disappointed → Stressed → Rushed → Defeated

**Opportunity Zones (where the system intervenes):**
- Stage 1: Show live queue status and item availability before leaving class
- Stage 3: Eliminate queue entirely with pre-ordering and token system
- Stage 4–5: Confirm item availability at order time
- Stage 6: Show accurate preparation time estimate
- Stage 7: Notify when order is ready so student can time their pickup

## 6. Key Insights Summary

| # | Insight | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Students lose 10–20 minutes daily to canteen queues during peak hours | Observation Sessions 1 & 2 |
| 2 | ~40% of observed students left without buying due to long queues | Observation Session 1 |
| 3 | Students have zero visibility into queue length, item availability, or prep time | All 5 interviews |
| 4 | Staff cannot predict demand, leading to ~15% daily food waste | Manager Interview |
| 5 | All interviewed students own smartphones and are familiar with food ordering apps | Interviews 1, 2, 3 |
| 6 | The peak hour problem is structural and daily — not random | Observations 1, 2, 3 |
| 7 | Even a 5-minute reduction in wait time would meaningfully change the experience | Interview 1, 2 |
| 8 | Canteen staff would benefit as much as students from a pre-order system | Interviews 4, 5 |
