# Interview Transcripts
## AI-Smart Ordering System — Primary Research

**Research Period:** 10–12 September 2026  
**Interviews Conducted:** 5  
**Method:** Structured 1:1 interviews, 7–12 minutes each

---

## Interview 1 — Student A (Priya R.)

**Profile:** 2nd Year, Computer Science Engineering, 20 years old  
**Interview Date:** 10 September 2026, 1:35 PM (immediately after emerging from canteen queue)  
**Duration:** ~12 minutes  
**Location:** College corridor outside canteen

---

**Q1: Can you describe what typically happens when you go to the canteen during lunch?**

> "I have class at 1:00 PM and my lunch break is only from 12:45 to 1:00 — that's literally 15 minutes. By the time I reach the canteen, the queue is already there. I spend 10 minutes just in the queue, then maybe 3 minutes getting food, and then I have to eat while walking to class or just skip eating. This happens almost every day."

---

**Q2: What is the most frustrating part of the canteen experience?**

> "Not knowing. I don't know how long the queue is until I actually get there. I don't know if the food I want is available. I've gone three times this week and twice what I wanted was sold out. But I still had to wait in the queue to find out — you only know when you reach the counter."

---

**Q3: Have you ever left the canteen without eating because of the queue?**

> "Yes, at least twice a week. Today I just bought biscuits from the store outside. It's sad because the canteen food is actually good and cheap. I'm spending more on outside food just to avoid waiting."

---

**Q4: If you could change one thing about the canteen experience, what would it be?**

> "I want to order before I leave class. Like Swiggy — I place the order, it's ready when I get there, I pick it up and go. No waiting in queue at all. Even 5 minutes saved would make a difference."

---

**Q5: Would you use a website or app to pre-order your food?**

> "Absolutely. 100%. Even if it's just a website on my phone. Anything is better than standing in a queue for 15 minutes when I only have 15 minutes break. I'd use it every single day."

---

**Q6: What features would matter most to you?**

> "First — knowing the food is actually available when I order, not finding out it's sold out after waiting. Second — knowing when to go to pick it up. Right now there's zero information. If I knew 'your food will be ready at 1:10 PM', I can plan my time."

## Interview 2 — Student B (Karthik S.)

**Profile:** 3rd Year, Electronics & Communication Engineering, 21 years old  
**Interview Date:** 10 September 2026, 2:00 PM  
**Duration:** ~10 minutes  
**Location:** College library entrance

---

**Q1: How often do you use the canteen, and what's your typical experience?**

> "Every day for lunch. Sometimes breakfast too if I have an 8 AM class. The experience is the same every time — queue. Sometimes short, sometimes very long. You never know what you're going to get."

---

**Q2: Can you describe a specific incident where the queue caused a real problem for you?**

> "Last week. I waited 20 minutes for chicken biryani, they said it was finished when I reached the counter. I had lab at 2 PM. I had to eat vada pav quickly from the stall outside and go to lab hungry. That affects concentration. The uncertainty is the real problem — not just the wait."

---

**Q3: Do you know what food will be available before you go?**

> "No. The menu is written on a blackboard but whether an item is actually available — you only know when you reach the counter. And by that time you've already waited. There should be some way to check before going."

---

**Q4: What would your ideal canteen experience look like?**

> "Order in the previous class itself. I get a token number. I go to the canteen at the time they tell me. I show my token, I take my food, I leave. No standing in a random queue. It's like how airport lounges work — you know your slot."

---

**Q5: What do you think would stop students from using a pre-ordering system?**

> "Nothing, if it's simple. If I have to download an app or create a complicated account it might put people off. But if it's just a website and I can order in 2 minutes? Everyone would use it."

## Interview 4 — Canteen Counter Staff (Rajan)

**Profile:** Counter staff, 35 years old, working at canteen for 6 years  
**Interview Date:** 12 September 2026, 3:15 PM (off-peak)  
**Duration:** ~7 minutes  
**Language:** Tamil (paraphrased in English below)

---

**Q1: What is your biggest challenge during peak hours?**

> "Too many students at the same time. Everyone wants different things. I have to take the order by listening, tell the kitchen, collect the cash, give change, and manage the crowd — all at the same time. I make mistakes. Wrong change, wrong item. I feel very stressed from 12:45 to 1:15 PM every day."

---

**Q2: Can you predict how many portions of each item you will need each day?**

> "No. We just guess. Sometimes we make 30 biryanis and 50 students want it. Sometimes we make 50 and only 20 students come. We throw away food or we make students wait extra while we prepare more. I don't know in advance."

---

**Q3: If students could order in advance through a phone or website, would that change your work?**

> "Very much yes. If I can see in the morning that 20 students want biryani at 1 PM, I tell the kitchen to prepare exactly 20. No waste. No shortage. No guessing. Right now every day is a guess."

---

**Q4: What would you need the system to show you?**

> "Which orders are ready to pick up. Which students are coming and when. Which items I need to prepare and how many. If I have this information on a screen, I can manage properly. Without information I am just reacting."

---

## Interview 5 — Canteen Manager (Name withheld by request)

**Profile:** Canteen manager, running this canteen for 8 years  
**Interview Date:** 12 September 2026, 3:30 PM  
**Duration:** ~10 minutes

---

**Q1: How do you currently manage the peak-hour demand?**

> "Experience. After 8 years I roughly know Monday lunch will be busier than Friday. But within a day, hour by hour — I cannot predict. I keep extra staff and hope for the best."

---

**Q2: What is the biggest operational problem you face?**

> "Two things. Food waste — we throw away 10–15% of what we prepare every day because we overprepare to avoid shortage. And student complaints — they say the wait is too long but what can I do if 200 students come at the same time? I have no tool to spread them out."

---

**Q3: Would a digital pre-ordering system change your operation?**

> "If students use it consistently, yes, completely. I can plan exactly how much to prepare for each hour. Kitchen staff can work in batches instead of random items. The counter staff do not need to take orders verbally — they just confirm and hand over. It removes the chaos. But students must actually use it, otherwise it doesn't help."

---

**Q4: What concerns you about implementing such a system?**

> "Training staff. Some of my staff are older and not very comfortable with technology. The system must be very simple. And what happens when internet is slow? There must be a backup way to handle orders."
